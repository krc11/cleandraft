Researchers can measure the way AI changed writing. One [study of more than 15 million PubMed abstracts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016) found hundreds of words appearing far more often in 2024 than earlier trends predicted, many of them style words like "delve". None of these words is wrong. The trouble is repetition: when every paragraph uses them, readers stop paying attention.

Here are the groups we see most often, and what to do with each.

## Filler openers

These words announce a connection instead of making one. If two sentences follow logically, the reader doesn't need a signpost.

| Stock opener | Try instead |
| --- | --- |
| Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally | Delete, or use "Also" now and then |
| Consequently, Therefore, Thus | "So" |
| Nevertheless, Nonetheless | "Still" or "Even so" |
| In conclusion, In summary | Delete, then just conclude |

## Throat-clearing

These phrases delay the point without adding to it. Cut them and start with what you meant to say.

- It is important to note that
- It is worth noting that
- It goes without saying that
- Needless to say

> **Before:** It is worth noting that the update fixes the login bug.
>
> **After:** The update fixes the login bug.

## Inflated phrases

| Stock phrase | Plain version |
| --- | --- |
| plays a crucial role in | is key to, matters for |
| a wide range of | many |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| has the ability to | can |
| at this point in time | now |
| when it comes to | for, with |

## Scene-setting clichés

"In today's fast-paced world" and "in the ever-evolving landscape of" could open almost any article. If a sentence would fit a thousand other pieces, delete it and open with the first thing specific to yours.

## Overused verbs and adjectives

Swap these only when the plain word says the same thing:

- **utilize, leverage:** use
- **delve into:** look at, dig into
- **foster:** build, encourage
- **robust:** strong
- **seamless:** smooth
- **pivotal, crucial:** key, central

Keep a technical term if your field uses it exactly. "Robust" means something specific in statistics, and swapping it there would be wrong.

## How to use this list

Don't turn it into a banned-word list. A single "moreover" in a long piece is fine. Search your draft for clusters, because that's where it starts to read like a template.
