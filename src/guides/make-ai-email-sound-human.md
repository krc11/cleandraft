---

Assistants are good at getting an email started and poor at sounding like you. Fortunately emails are short, and most of the problem sits in three predictable places.

## Fix the opening

Delete "I hope this email finds you well" unless you really do say that. Open with the reason you're writing.

> **Before:** I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to provide an update regarding the Q3 budget.
>
> **After:** The Q3 budget is ready, and I need your sign-off by Thursday.

## Trim the middle

Replace formal connectors with the words you would use out loud.

| AI email | Spoken version |
| --- | --- |
| regarding | about |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| please be advised that | (delete) |
| I wanted to reach out to | I'm writing to, or just say it |

Then cut every sentence the reader already knows. People skim email, and a two-line message that gets read beats a ten-line one that doesn't.

## Fix the sign-off

"Please do not hesitate to reach out if you have any questions" is a polite way of saying nothing. End with the next step: who does what, and by when.

> **Before:** Please do not hesitate to reach out should you require any further information.
>
> **After:** If Thursday won't work, tell me and I'll move the review.

## Match the reader

Tone depends on who's reading. In [CleanDraft's email editor](/ai-email-editor), Professional tone adds some contractions, Natural adds more, and Formal removes them. Choose by asking how you would speak to this person in a meeting.

## A full example

> **Before:** I hope this email finds you well. Furthermore, I wanted to reach out to you regarding the upcoming client presentation. It is important to note that the deadline has been moved. Please do not hesitate to reach out if you have any questions.
>
> **After:** The client presentation deadline has moved to Tuesday, May 12. Can you send me your slides by Monday noon? If that's too tight, let me know today and I'll ask for more time.

The tool can do part of this: it removes the stock phrases and simplifies the wording. The date and the direct request came from a person. Those are what make the email work.

## Check before sending

- Is the request in the first two lines?
- Are names, dates and amounts right?
- Would you say the first sentence out loud?
