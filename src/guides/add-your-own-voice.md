---
title: "How to add your own voice to an AI draft"
description: "Five exercises that bring your personality back into AI-assisted writing, plus how to build a personal style sheet."
order: 6
---

"Voice" sounds mysterious, but most of it is just habits: the words you pick, the opinions you hold and the examples you reach for. You can put those back on purpose.

## Dictate the hard parts

Close the draft and explain the idea aloud, as if to a friend. Record it on your phone, then transcribe it. Your spoken version will be messier, more direct and much more like you. Use its best lines to replace the draft's.

## State what you actually think

Find every hedge such as "there are pros and cons" or "it depends on your needs". Replace at least one with what you would really advise.

> **Before:** Both approaches offer unique benefits, and the right choice depends on your needs.
>
> **After:** For a team under ten people, I'd pick the simpler tool every time. You can switch later, and you probably won't need to.

## Swap in your own examples

The generic example is the most obvious sign of a draft nobody lived through. Replace it with one from your own work, your industry or your week. A small real detail beats a big imaginary one.

## Break a rule you like

Everyone has habits. You might start sentences with "And", use very short paragraphs, or make a dry joke in the second line. If you do these things naturally, put them back. Assistants smooth such quirks away, and they're what readers recognize.

## Compare with your old writing

Pick something you wrote before you started using AI. Read it, then read your draft. Notice:

- How long are your sentences compared with the draft's?
- Do you use contractions?
- How do you open a paragraph?
- What words does the draft use that you never would?

## Build a style sheet

Turn what you find into a short list you can reuse:

| Item | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Words I never use | "leverage", "utilize", "synergy" |
| Words I do use | "honestly", "the catch is" |
| How I open | With the point, not with context |
| How I close | With the next step |
| Contractions? | Yes, always |

Run your draft through the [editor](/) to clear the stock phrasing first, so you aren't fixing small things by hand. Then use your style sheet for what a rules engine can't know: how you sound.
