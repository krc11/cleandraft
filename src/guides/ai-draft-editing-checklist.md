---
title: "A 10-point checklist for editing an AI draft"
description: "Work through an AI draft in the right order, from checking facts to the final read-aloud, without wasting time polishing weak sections."
order: 3
---

Order matters when you edit a draft. There is no point polishing a sentence in a section you're about to delete. Work from the biggest questions down to the smallest.

## Check the substance first

1. **Is it true?** Check every fact, figure, quote and source yourself. AI drafts can state wrong details with total confidence, and invented citations are a known problem. No editing tool can catch this.
2. **Does it answer the real question?** Write down in one sentence what the reader needs. Delete anything that doesn't serve it.
3. **Is anything missing?** Assistants don't know your context. Add the constraint, the number, or the awkward truth that only you know.

## Fix the structure

4. **Lead with the point.** AI drafts often warm up first. Move your main message to the top, and delete the scene-setting.
5. **Remove repetition.** Conclusions that repeat the introduction, and section endings that restate the section, can usually go.
6. **Add one real example per section.** Replace "many businesses" with a named case, a date or a result you can stand behind.

## Clean the language

7. **Run a stock-phrase pass.** Use [CleanDraft](/) at Balanced, then read the edit log. Reject any swap that doesn't fit by pressing "This edit is wrong".
8. **Vary your rhythm by hand.** Find three sentences in a row of similar length. Split one or merge two.

## Finish like a person

9. **Add your opinion.** Say what you'd actually recommend, and why. Taking a position is the fastest way to stop sounding like a template.
10. **Read it aloud.** Fix everything you stumble on. If you wouldn't say it, change it.

## How to split your time

As a rule of thumb, for a 1,000-word draft:

| Stage | Share of your time |
| --- | --- |
| Facts and substance (1 to 3) | About half |
| Structure (4 to 6) | About a third |
| Language and final read (7 to 10) | The rest |

Notice that language comes last and takes the least time. Tools make that stage fast, so spend the time you save on the first two stages, where they can't help.
