"Humanizing" AI text means editing a draft from ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini so that it reads as if a person wrote it with care. You can do it by hand in a few minutes. Here is the method we use, in the order that saves the most time.

## Step 1: Check the facts before the wording

There's no point polishing a sentence that's wrong. AI drafts can state false details with confidence and can invent sources. Check every figure, name, quote and link first. No humanizer, ours included, can do this for you.

## Step 2: Cut the warm-up and the wrap-up

AI drafts tend to open by restating the topic and close by summarizing what they just said. Delete the first sentence or two and see whether anything is lost. Do the same with the last paragraph. Often the draft gets better.

## Step 3: Remove filler openers and stock phrases

This is the most mechanical step. Look for:

- Openers such as "Moreover," "Furthermore," and "Additionally,"
- Throat-clearing such as "It is important to note that"
- Inflated phrases such as "plays a crucial role in" and "a wide range of"

Delete them or swap in a plain word. Our list of [AI phrases to avoid](/guides/ai-phrases-to-avoid) has the common ones with replacements, and the [CleanDraft editor](/) does this step automatically and shows each change.

## Step 4: Use plain words and contractions

Change "utilize" to "use" and "in order to" to "to". Then add contractions where you'd say them aloud: "don't", "it's", "we're". This one change does a lot to remove the stiff tone. Keep the full forms for formal documents.

## Step 5: Vary the rhythm

AI sentences are often about the same length, one after another. Find three in a row that match. Cut one short. Join two others, or turn one into a question. Reading aloud makes the flat spots easy to hear.

## Step 6: Add what only you know

This step matters most, and no tool can do it. For each paragraph, add one thing the AI couldn't know: a number from your own work, a name, a date, something that went wrong, or what you'd actually recommend. If a paragraph could appear in anyone's article unchanged, add to it or cut it.

## Step 7: Read it aloud once

Anything you stumble over, or wouldn't say to a colleague, gets changed. Then you're done.

## A worked example

> **AI draft:** In today's fast-paced world, effective time management plays a crucial role in productivity. Moreover, it is important to note that individuals who utilize a wide range of planning tools are able to achieve better outcomes.

After steps 2 to 4:

> **Cleaner:** Time management matters for productivity. People who use planning tools get more done.

After steps 5 and 6:

> **Yours:** I used to plan my day in three apps. Now I use one paper list with five lines on it, and I finish more.

The middle version is what a rules-based tool can produce. The last one needs you. (It's an illustration: write your own, and make it true.)

## Which steps a tool can do

| Step | Tool or you? |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Check facts | You |
| 2. Cut warm-up and wrap-up | You |
| 3. Remove stock phrases | Tool |
| 4. Plain words and contractions | Tool |
| 5. Vary rhythm | Mostly you |
| 6. Add your own detail | You |
| 7. Read aloud | You |

A tool saves time on steps 3 and 4 so you can spend it on step 6, which is where a draft starts to sound like a person.

## Use it honestly

Humanizing a draft makes it read better. It doesn't change who wrote it. If your school, employer or publisher has rules about AI use or disclosure, they still apply. See [how to use and disclose AI honestly](/guides/disclose-ai-use).
