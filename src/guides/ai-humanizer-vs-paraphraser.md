Humanizers, paraphrasers and grammar checkers all change your text, so they're easy to confuse. They solve different problems. Using the wrong one wastes time and can make a draft worse.

## The quick comparison

| Tool | What it's for | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grammar checker | Fixing errors | Spelling, grammar, punctuation |
| Paraphraser | Saying the same thing in different words | Most of the wording and structure |
| AI humanizer | Making AI text read naturally | Tone, stock phrases, rhythm |

## Grammar checkers

A grammar checker finds mistakes: a missing comma, a verb that doesn't agree, a misspelled word. It assumes your wording is what you meant and tries to make it correct.

**Use it when** the draft says what you want but may have errors. It's the right last step for almost any writing.

**It won't help when** the text is correct but stiff. AI drafts usually have clean grammar already, so a grammar checker finds little to fix in them.

## Paraphrasers

A paraphraser rewrites a passage in new words while keeping the meaning. Sentences are restructured and vocabulary is swapped throughout.

**Use it when** you need to restate something: simplifying a dense paragraph, or putting a source's idea in your own words before citing it.

**Watch for two things.** A paraphraser can shift the meaning, so compare the result with the original. And paraphrasing a source doesn't remove the need to cite it. Reworded ideas still belong to whoever had them.

## AI humanizers

A humanizer targets the habits that make AI drafts sound alike: filler openers, inflated phrases, a formal tone with no contractions, and even sentence rhythm. Some humanizers are AI rewriters, which behave much like paraphrasers. Others, like [CleanDraft](/), are rule-based and change only the patterns they recognize. Our guide to [what an AI humanizer is](/guides/what-is-an-ai-humanizer) covers the two types.

**Use it when** the draft came from an assistant and is accurate but reads flat.

**It won't help when** the draft has factual errors or nothing specific to say.

## Which one do you need?

| Your problem | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "There may be typos and grammar slips." | Grammar checker |
| "This paragraph is too dense." | Paraphraser, or rewrite by hand |
| "I need to restate a source." | Paraphrase by hand, and cite it |
| "My AI draft sounds stiff and generic." | Humanizer |
| "My English isn't idiomatic." | Grammar checker, and a fluent reader if you can |
| "The draft has no real examples." | None of them. Add your own |

## A sensible order

If you use more than one, the order matters:

1. **Get the content right.** Check facts and add your own detail.
2. **Humanize.** Clear out the stock phrasing.
3. **Edit by hand.** Fix rhythm and add your voice.
4. **Grammar check last.** It catches slips you introduced while editing.

Running a grammar checker first means doing it twice, because every later edit can add new errors.

## What none of them do

No tool in this list checks whether your text is true, and none can add what you know. They work on wording. The parts of writing that readers value most, such as a clear point and real experience, still come from the writer.
