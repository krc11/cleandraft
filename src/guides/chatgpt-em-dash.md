The em dash (—) has become the punctuation mark people most associate with AI writing. Drafts from ChatGPT and other assistants often use several in a paragraph, where most people would use one in a page. Here's what's going on and what to do about it.

## What an em dash is

It's the long dash used to set off an aside or add a punchy ending to a sentence. It's different from the shorter en dash (–), used in ranges like 2019–2024, and from the hyphen (-), used inside words like "well-known".

| Mark | Name | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| — | Em dash | An aside, or a break in thought |
| – | En dash | Ranges of numbers or dates |
| - | Hyphen | Joining words |

## Why AI drafts use so many

Nobody outside the companies that build these models can say for certain. The likely explanation is simple. Assistants learn from large amounts of edited, published writing, such as books, magazines and essays, where em dashes are common. Most everyday writing, like emails and messages, uses far fewer, partly because the mark isn't on a standard keyboard. So an assistant's "default voice" has more dashes than most people's.

The dash is also handy for a model. It can join almost any two ideas without choosing a more exact link like "because" or "but".

## Does an em dash prove AI wrote something?

No. Writers used em dashes heavily long before AI. Emily Dickinson's poems are famous for them, and many journalists and novelists love the mark. Plenty of people type them every day.

A draft with a dash in every other sentence may feel machine-written to readers who have learned the pattern. That's a reason to edit your own drafts. It isn't evidence about anyone else's.

## Three ways to replace them

Each dash is doing one of three jobs, and each job has a plainer option.

**1. A pair of dashes around an aside: use commas.**

> **Before:** The launch went well — better than we expected — and the team is ready for the next phase.
>
> **After:** The launch went well, better than we expected, and the team is ready for the next phase.

**2. A dash before a new full thought: start a new sentence.**

> **Before:** We tested the new layout for a month — it cut support tickets in half.
>
> **After:** We tested the new layout for a month. It cut support tickets in half.

**3. A dash before an explanation or list: use a colon.**

> **Before:** The plan needs one thing — a clear owner.
>
> **After:** The plan needs one thing: a clear owner.

## How to find and replace them quickly

- **In Word or Google Docs:** open Find and paste an em dash into the search box, then step through each one. Don't use Replace All. The right fix depends on the job the dash is doing.
- **In ChatGPT:** ask it not to use em dashes. This helps, though assistants don't always follow the instruction over a long reply.
- **In CleanDraft:** the [editor](/) turns paired dashes into commas and a single dash into a comma or a new sentence, at Balanced strength and above. Each change appears in the edit log, so you can undo any you'd like to keep.

## How to type one, if you want it

Used once in a while, the em dash is a good mark. To type it:

| System | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Windows | Hold Alt and type 0151 on the number pad |
| Mac | Option + Shift + Hyphen |
| iPhone and Android | Hold the hyphen key and pick the long dash |
| Word and Google Docs | Type two hyphens between words and keep typing |

## The short version

Keep a dash where it earns its place, maybe once or twice in a piece. Replace the rest with a comma, a full stop or a colon. Your sentences will be clearer for it, whoever wrote the first draft.
