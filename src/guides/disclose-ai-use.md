Rules on AI vary widely, and they change quickly. What's fine in one class may be misconduct in the next. This guide is general information, not a statement of your school's or employer's policy, so always check the actual rules that apply to you.

## Check the rules first

Before you use AI on anything you'll submit, find out:

- **At school:** the course syllabus, the assignment brief and your institution's academic integrity policy. If they're unclear, ask your instructor, and do it in writing.
- **At work:** your employer's AI policy, and client contracts, which may restrict it. Check whether you may paste confidential information into any tool at all.

If you can't find a rule, asking is always safer than guessing.

## Understand where the line is

A rough way to think about it is how much of the thinking is yours.

| Use | How it's often treated |
| --- | --- |
| Checking spelling and grammar in your own text | Usually allowed |
| Brainstorming or outlining | Depends on the course or employer |
| Editing a draft you wrote | Depends, and it may need disclosure |
| Submitting AI-generated text as your own | Often prohibited unless expressly permitted |

Passing an AI's work off as your own, where that's not allowed, is dishonest whichever tools you use afterward. Editing doesn't change who did the thinking.

## How to disclose

If disclosure is required or welcome, say plainly which tool you used and what for:

> I used ChatGPT to suggest an outline and CleanDraft to tidy the wording of my own draft. I wrote the arguments and checked all sources myself.

## How to cite

Major style guides have published guidance on citing generative AI. Check the current advice from [APA](https://apastyle.apa.org/), [MLA](https://style.mla.org/) or [Chicago](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/), and follow whichever your instructor or publisher requires. In general, you'll name the tool, the company, the version or date, and sometimes the prompt.

## Keep a record

Good records protect you if questions come up later:

- Keep your notes, outlines and earlier drafts.
- Turn on version history in your writing tool.
- Save any prompts you used.
- Save your sources, and read them. AI tools can invent citations.

## If your work is questioned

AI detectors can be wrong, and that includes wrongly flagging human writing. If you are accused, stay calm, share your drafts and notes, and offer to talk through your reasoning. Your school's policy should describe the process.
