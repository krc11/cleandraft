Plain language means choosing the word your reader understands fastest. Government style guides such as the [Australian Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/) recommend common, shorter words for this reason. AI drafts lean the other way and often pick the formal option.

## When to keep the formal word

Plain doesn't always mean better. Keep the original when:

- **It's a term of art.** "Terminate" has a precise meaning in employment law and in computing. "Methodology" is standard in a research paper's section heading.
- **The meanings differ.** "Mitigate" doesn't always mean "reduce". "Possess" has a legal sense that "have" lacks.
- **Your reader expects it.** Contracts, regulatory filings and some academic writing follow their own conventions.
- **Quoted or defined language is involved.** Don't "simplify" what someone said.

CleanDraft's rules have guards for some of these cases, and it never touches quoted text. Still, the [edit log](/how-it-works) is there because a rule can't always tell. If a swap is wrong for your field, revert it.

## Try it

Pick a paragraph you wrote today and swap every word in the tables that you can. Read both versions aloud. You'll probably find that the plain one says the same thing and seems more confident. [The editor](/) can do this pass for you at Balanced strength.
