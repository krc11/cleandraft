AI can be a real help when you're writing in a second language. It can also take your voice away, or leave you sending text you can't quite judge. Here's how to get the benefits while keeping control.

## Write first, then ask for help

If you can, write your rough version first, even if it's imperfect. Then ask an assistant to correct it rather than generate it. You'll keep your ideas and your way of putting them, and you'll learn more from the corrections.

## Know what a rules editor can fix

Let's be direct: CleanDraft isn't the best tool for fixing literal or unidiomatic English. It finds stock phrases, not translation errors.

| Problem | Will CleanDraft fix it? |
| --- | --- |
| Too-formal AI wording | Yes, often |
| Repeated stock phrases | Yes |
| Grammar errors | No, use a grammar checker |
| Phrasing translated literally from your language | No |
| Wrong word with a similar meaning | No |

## Learn from the edit log

Each log entry shows a before, an after and a reason, which makes it a small English lesson. If you see "utilize" become "use" ten times, you've learned something about tone. Keep a list of swaps you want to remember.

## Choose the right level of formality

Formality varies between cultures. Very polite phrasing in your language can sound stiff or even cold in English workplaces, especially in the US, Canada and Australia.

> **Very formal:** I would be grateful if you could kindly provide the document at your earliest convenience.
>
> **Normal at work:** Could you send me the document by Friday?

Contractions like "don't" and "I'll" are normal in most work emails. If in doubt, try the Professional tone in [the editor](/), and compare it with Formal.

## Check that it still says what you mean

Whenever you use an AI draft, check these things before sending:

- Do you understand every sentence? If not, look it up or replace it.
- Could you explain the message in your own words if someone asked?
- Are names, numbers and dates correct?
- Is the level of certainty right? "We will" and "we might" are very different promises.

## Choose your spelling

If you're writing for a particular country, set the English option. CleanDraft can match US, UK, Canadian or Australian spelling, so "colour" and "color" stay consistent throughout the text.

## Don't lose your accent on the page

Your English doesn't have to sound as if it came from nowhere. A direct style or an unusual turn of phrase can be part of what makes your writing yours. Aim for clear and correct, not for sounding like everybody else.
