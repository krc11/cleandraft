The introduction is where readers decide whether to stay, and it's where AI drafts are at their weakest. A typical one says the topic is important, that things are changing, and that this article will explore it. None of that gives anyone a reason to read on.

## Step 1: Delete the first two sentences

Do this nearly every time. AI drafts tend to warm up before they say anything, so the real start is often sentence three.

## Step 2: Write down what the reader wants

In one line, finish this: "My reader is here because..." Be specific. "Their cold emails aren't getting replies" is more useful than "they're interested in email".

## Step 3: Pick an opening

Four openings work reliably:

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The problem | "Your cold emails are getting opened and ignored." |
| The result | "One change doubled our reply rate in a month." |
| The disagreement | "Personalizing every email is mostly a waste of time." |
| The story | "My worst email went to 400 people, and the merge field was broken." |

The examples show the form. Use only claims and stories that are true for you.

## Step 4: Promise something concrete

Say what the reader will be able to do at the end, and skip "in this article, we will explore".

> **Before:** In this article, we will delve into the various strategies that can help you improve your cold emails.
>
> **After:** Below are the three changes we made, and the template we use now.

## Step 5: Cut until it hurts

Most introductions need only a few sentences. If yours runs longer, check whether the last sentence could be the first. Often it can.

## Worked example

> **AI draft:** In today's fast-paced business world, effective communication plays a crucial role in the success of any organization. Email remains a vital tool. In this article, we will explore key strategies for writing better emails.

> **Edited:** Nobody reads your long emails. Here are three ways to get the same message across in five lines, and a template you can copy.

An editor can clean up the first version, turning "plays a crucial role" into "is key". It still won't be a good introduction, because the problem is what it says, not how it says it. Rewrite your introduction by hand, then use [CleanDraft](/) on the rest.
